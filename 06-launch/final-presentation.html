GTM Strategy

The organizer is always the bank

One friend fronts thousands for the group trip, chases everyone for weeks, and eats the loss when someone drops out. 43% of US travelers have lost $1,000+ this way.

Who

  • The Designated Organizer, 26–38
  • Plans 1–3 trips a year for 4–12 friends
  • Bookings of €1.5k–€8k

Motion

  • Product-led core: entry on every 4+-guest search
  • Invite links are the growth loop
  • Marketing-led seasonal pushes: planning season, bach season

Success at 90 days

  • 5,000 fully-paid group bookings / month
  • ≥ 85% shares paid in the hold window
  • ≥ 1.5 new accounts per booking

Riskiest assumption: friends pay on time. Tested first with a 50-organizer concierge pilot.

The bet

The organizer, not the destination, is the unit of growth. Remove their risk and every trip brings 3–11 new travelers at near-zero CAC.

The question to plant

"What happens to the booking if one person doesn't pay on time?"

Not doing: corporate travel, wedding blocks, flights, groups over 16.

Positioning

Nobody's the bank

For friend-group trip organizers who are tired of fronting thousands and chasing friends for money, Roamly Groups is the group trip booking experience that lets everyone vote on the stay and pay their own share at checkout. Unlike booking on one card and collecting later, it keeps the booking safe even when someone flakes.

Category

Group trip booking

Differentiator

Dropout-proof split: the booking holds when someone is late

Core truth

Pricing Recommendation

Free core, per-traveler upgrade

Hybrid model: standard commission on every share keeps the loop free; the organizer chooses Plus, and its cost is split across the group.

Launch & Individual Insights

What I learned

Launch plan

  • Pre-launch: fake-door test, 50-organizer pilot, host opt-in for holds
  • Launch: January planning season, creator campaign, in-app entry
  • Post-launch: roll out Plus, pricing research, turn invitees into organizers
  • Tripwire: share completion under 70% for 2 weeks pauses expansion

Insights

  • Aha: the buyer and the payer are different people — it shaped messaging, pricing and growth
  • Friction: positioning is easy to copy until you name what competitors can't do
  • Takeaway: study failed attempts, not only current players
  • Monday: name the one assumption that breaks the plan, test it first
