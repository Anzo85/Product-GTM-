## 1. Launch plan

| Phase | Goal | Key activities | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pre-launch** (weeks −10 to 0) | Prove the riskiest assumptions: friends pay on time, hosts accept holds. | Fake-door test on 4+-guest searches; concierge pilot with 50 organizers in two markets; host opt-in for payment holds; Plus price A/B; seed 10–15 trip-planning creators; build waitlist. | PM, Supply lead, PMM |
| **Launch** (January planning season) | Put Groups in front of every organizer at the moment of intent. | Groups entry point on all 4+-guest searches; creator campaign around bach, birthday and ski trips; PR story on the "organizer tax"; invitee welcome credit toward their own first trip. | PMM, Growth |
| **Post-launch** (weeks 1–12) | Tune the loop and start monetizing. | Optimize invite-to-pay funnel; roll out Groups Plus; run Van Westendorp and Max-Diff; lifecycle emails that turn invitees into organizers; scope Groups Host. | PM, Growth, Finance |

## 2. Launch metrics & guardrails

- **Success looks like:** ≥ 85% share completion within the hold window; ≥ 1.5 new accounts per group booking; ≥ 15% of 4+-guest searches start a Group; 5,000 fully-paid group bookings per month by day 90.
- **Tripwire (when to pull back):** Share completion below 70% for two consecutive weeks, gaps covered by organizers on more than 10% of bookings, or host cancellations of held bookings above 5%. Any one of these pauses market expansion until fixed.

## 3. The full GTM story in one slide

Organizers front thousands for every group trip and absorb the loss when friends flake (**M1: the problem and ICP**). The biggest platform tried to fix it with an all-or-nothing split and dropped it, leaving the job unserved (**M2: the gap and the bet** — the organizer is the unit of growth). So we position Groups as the booking where nobody's the bank and the booking never collapses (**M3**), tell organizers "plan the trip, not the payback" and invitees "pay your share in a minute" (**M4**), keep the core free so the invite loop runs, and monetize with a per-traveler Plus that spreads cost across the group (**M5**). We launch in planning season, behind a pilot that proves friends actually pay (**M6**).

## 4. Individual insights

> ✏️ _Draft — rewrite in your own voice before submitting. These are prompts based on how this project came together; keep what's true for you and replace the rest._

- **Biggest "aha" across the certification:** The buyer and the payer aren't the same person. The organizer chooses Roamly; the invitees pay. That single observation shaped the messaging (two pillars), the pricing (per-traveler, added to each share) and the growth model (invitees as the acquisition channel).
- **What changed in how you think about GTM:** Competitive research is more useful when it looks for *failed* attempts, not just current players. Airbnb shipping and removing split payments told me more about the design constraint (don't let one late payer kill the booking) than any feature comparison.
- **What you'd do differently with more time / data:** Talk to 10 real organizers before writing the positioning, and find out why the Airbnb feature was discontinued instead of treating it only as an opportunity.
- **The one idea you'll apply at work on Monday:** Write down the one assumption that breaks the strategy if it's wrong — and test it before building anything.
