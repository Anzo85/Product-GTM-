## 1. The competitive set

| Competitor | What they do well | Where they're weak | How we win |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The status quo:** one card + group chat + payment apps / spreadsheet | Free, familiar, infinitely flexible. Nobody has to learn anything. | The organizer fronts everything and carries all the risk. Weeks of chasing. Dropouts cost the organizer real money. | Remove the fronting entirely: the organizer's card only ever pays the organizer's share. |
| **Big booking platforms** (single-payer checkout) | Huge inventory, trust, reviews, loyalty. | Checkout assumes one payer. Airbnb tried split payments in 2017 with a strict all-or-nothing 72-hour rule (booking forfeited if anyone missed it) and the feature has since been discontinued — leaving the job unserved. | A split designed around the organizer: a late payer never kills the booking; the gap is re-offered to the group or covered by choice. |
| **Group trip planners** (e.g. collaborative itinerary / voting apps) | Great at the fun part: shortlists, polls, shared itineraries. | Decisions leave the app — the group still books and pays elsewhere, so the money problem is untouched. _(Verify each competitor's current feature set before submission.)_ | Plan → vote → book → pay in one flow, on real inventory. |

## 2. Battlecard

- **Our one-line advantage:** The only way to book a group stay where everyone pays their own share *and* the booking can't collapse because one friend is late.
- **Trap to set / question to plant:** *"What happens to the booking if one person doesn't pay on time?"* — on the status quo, the organizer eats it; on an all-or-nothing split, everyone loses the place.
- **Objection → response:**
  - _"I'll just pay and get paid back — it's fine."_ → You can. But you're lending thousands, interest-free, to people who sometimes flake. With Groups your card only pays your share, and it takes friends about a minute.
  - _"What if someone doesn't pay — do we lose the place?"_ → No. The booking stands. Unpaid shares are re-offered to the group, or you choose to cover them. Nobody loses the house.
  - _"My friends won't download another app."_ → They don't have to. Invitees pay from a link with Apple Pay, Google Pay or card.

## 3. The strategic bet

> We are betting that **the organizer — not the destination — is the unit of growth in group travel**, because **removing the organizer's financial risk will make them plan more and bigger trips on Roamly, and every trip onboards 3–11 new travelers at near-zero CAC.**

- **Why now:** The category leader has exited the split-payment job, leaving it open. Paying your own share at the point of purchase is now normal for dinners and rides — travel hasn't caught up. Link-based checkout and mobile wallets make multi-party payment cheap to build.
- **What we're deliberately NOT doing:** Corporate/MICE group travel, wedding room blocks, flights or packages, and groups over 16 in V1. We win one job for one person first.
- **What would prove the bet right within 90 days:** ≥ 85% share completion in the hold window, ≥ 1.5 new accounts per group booking, and group bookings averaging ≥ 3× the value of solo bookings.

## 4. Evidence

- **Proven demand:** Split payments was one of the top feature requests when Airbnb's CEO asked users what to build (2017).
- **Proven loop:** Airbnb reported that 30% of reservations using its split tool during testing brought in one or more new users.
- **Proven pain:** An Airbnb-commissioned survey (DKC Analytics, 2,000 US adults) found 79% had taken a group trip in five years and 43% had lost $1,000+ to companions not paying their share.
- **Open gap:** The feature was discontinued — the job is unserved by the largest player. _To validate:_ why it was dropped, and Roamly's own share of searches with 4+ guests.
