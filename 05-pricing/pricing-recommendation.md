# Pricing & Packaging Recommendation — Roamly Groups

> Module 5 · Increase Revenue Through Smarter Pricing and Packaging — ★ Deliverable 5
>
> Propose a pricing model and packaging structure for a product with no existing price. Value first, packaging second, research last — then a one-page recommendation for stakeholders.

## 1. Pricing goal

- **Goal:** Enter a new market (friend-group leisure bookings) and maximize adoption — penetration first, monetization second.
- **Why this is the right commercial goal now:** The M2 bet is that organizers are the unit of growth. Every group booking already earns Roamly's standard commission on 4–16 shares and brings new users, so the core feature should never have a paywall in front of it. Charging for the core would tax the loop that makes the bet pay off.

## 2. Pricing model

- **Model:** **Hybrid** — transaction-based (standard booking commission on every share, free core) + an optional per-trip upgrade priced per traveler.
- **Who pays — and for what?** The buyer and the payer are different people. The **organizer** decides to upgrade (they feel the value: longer holds, uneven splits, reminders). The **invitees** pay the booking. So the Plus fee is charged **per traveler, added to each share** — the organizer makes the call, but the cost is spread across the group and never falls on one person. That's consistent with the whole promise: nobody's the bank.

Subscription was rejected: organizers book 1–3 group trips a year, so value arrives per trip, not per month.

## 3. Packaging approach

- **Structure:** By usage limits (group size, hold length) + features.
- **Audiences & fit:** The M4 audiences (organizer, invitee) share one structure — the invitee never makes a pricing decision. Tiers separate organizers by trip size and complexity.

| Tier | Who it's for | Price | What's included |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Groups** | Casual organizers, simple weekend trips | Free (standard booking commission) | Up to 8 travelers; shortlist and voting; split at checkout; 48-hour hold; late shares re-split or covered — booking never collapses. |
| **Groups Plus** ★ | The Designated Organizer: bach parties, birthdays, ski weeks | €3 per traveler per trip (min €12), added to each share | Up to 16 travelers; 7-day hold (where the host allows); automatic reminders; uneven splits (the big bedroom pays more); instalments (30% now, rest 30 days before check-in); priority support. |
| **Groups Host** _(post-launch)_ | Repeat organizers of large groups: clubs, retreats | €79 per trip | 17–30 travelers; organizer dashboard; custom deposits and deadlines; multiple properties; collect for add-on activities. |

## 4. Research to validate

- **Method:** **Van Westendorp** on the Plus per-traveler fee (is €3 inside the acceptable range?), paired with **Max-Diff** on Plus features (which of longer holds, uneven splits, instalments and reminders actually drives the upgrade?). Recruit organizers who booked a 4+ person trip in the last 12 months.
- **Assumption to validate:** Organizers will upgrade — and pass a small per-traveler fee to friends — for longer holds and uneven splits, without it lowering share completion.
- **The one thing that would change everything if we got it wrong:** If hosts won't accept payment holds, the core mechanic breaks and pricing is irrelevant. This must be validated with supply before launch.

## 5. The one-page recommendation

> We recommend **a free core with an optional €3-per-traveler Groups Plus upgrade** for Roamly Groups, to **win the friend-group segment and grow through the invite loop**, because **the core feature already earns commission on every share, and charging per traveler spreads the upgrade cost across the group instead of back onto the organizer.**

- **Finance** (margin & revenue model): Group bookings carry ~4× the value of a solo booking at the same take rate (planning assumption: ~€2,400 vs ~€600). Plus is near-pure software margin: a typical 8-person Plus trip adds €24. New users from the loop are acquired at ~€0.
- **Sales Leadership** (it closes deals): For a consumer product, "sales" is supply partnerships — pitch hosts that group bookings mean bigger stays, fewer no-shows and paid-in-full reservations, in exchange for enabling holds.
- **Product Marketing** (the story that lands): Free is the hook — "Book together. Pay separately." Plus is the upgrade story for the organizer who's done this before: "Bigger group, longer hold, fairer split."

**Validate first:** Run an A/B test in the pilot — Plus offered at €3 vs. no Plus offered — measuring upgrade rate and, critically, whether the fee changes share-completion rate.
