**Product in one line:** Roamly Groups lets a group of friends shortlist and vote on a stay together, then book it with everyone paying their own share at checkout — and the booking holds even if someone flakes.

## 1. The Discover Framework

## 2. The problem & the audience

- **Problem worth solving:** Booking a group stay forces one person to lend thousands, interest-free, to their friends — then spend weeks chasing repayment, absorbing the loss if someone drops out. It also caps the group's options at what one person's card can carry.
- **Primary ICP:** The Designated Organizer (above). Secondary audience: the **invitees** — they pay, and many become new Roamly users.
- **What they do today (the alternative):** Plan in a group chat, track in a spreadsheet, book on one card at a booking site, then collect via payment apps and reminders.

## 3. GTM motion

**Chosen motion:** Hybrid — **product-led** core with **marketing-led** seasonal amplification.

Groups is a free feature inside an app people already use, the purchase is a consumer decision made in minutes, and the product spreads itself: every booking pushes Roamly into 3–11 new inboxes. A sales-led motion makes no sense at this price point. Marketing's job is to create the moments (planning season, bach season) that trigger the first Group.

- **Acquisition:** In-app entry point on every 4+-guest search; invite links as the growth loop; creator and SEO content for organizers not yet on Roamly.
- **Activation:** A Group is "activated" when all shares are paid and the booking is confirmed. Time-to-activation target: under 48 hours.
- **Monetization:** Standard booking commission on every share (free core), plus the optional per-trip **Groups Plus** upgrade (see Module 5).

## 4. V1 one-pager

> For **friend-group organizers** who **are tired of fronting the whole trip and chasing everyone for money**, Roamly Groups is a **group trip booking experience** that **lets everyone vote on the stay and pay their own share at checkout**. Unlike **booking on one card and collecting later through payment apps**, we **keep the booking safe even when someone flakes — so the organizer is never the bank.**

## 5. Success metrics

| Metric | Target (90 days post-launch) | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| _North-star:_ Fully-paid group bookings per month | 5,000 across pilot markets | Captures the whole job — decided, paid, confirmed — not vanity invites. |
| _Leading:_ Share-completion rate within hold window | ≥ 85% | If friends don't pay, nothing else works. |
| _Leading:_ New Roamly accounts per group booking | ≥ 1.5 | Proves the invite loop is an acquisition channel. |
| _Leading:_ 4+-guest searches that start a Group | ≥ 15% | Proves organizers see and want the feature. |

_Targets are planning assumptions to be recalibrated after the pilot._

## 6. The assumption I'm most worried about

**That invitees will pay their share inside the hold window at a high rate (≥ 85%).** If they don't, the organizer ends up covering gaps anyway and we've just added steps.

**Test:** A concierge pilot with 50 organizers before building the full flow — real bookings, payment links sent manually, deadlines and reminders run by the team. Measure completion rate, time-to-pay, and where people drop off.
